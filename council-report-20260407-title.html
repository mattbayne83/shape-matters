Key Tensions

  • Question vs. Command: Executor wants "Build Your Signal Profile" (directive). Council says the question creates a productive beat of discomfort.
  • "Your Org" vs. "Your Structure": Warmer vs. more precise. Verdict: precision wins — four sections trained users to think structurally.

Blind Spots Caught in Peer Review

1. Subtitle should preview the slider labels. Users need to know what they're about to do — "depth, headcount, fidelity, cycle time, adaptability" should appear so the interaction feels concrete before they touch anything.
2. User emotional state: "implicated, seeking agency." After four accusatory sections, the tool is the reward. Title should offer agency through interaction, not pile on more evidence.
3. "What Shape Are You?" breaks the tool's output. The tool produces three pillar scores, not a shape classification. The title would promise something the tool doesn't deliver — confusion at the moment you need trust.

Advisor Responses

The Contrarian — "How Much of Your Strategy Actually Lands?"

"Look Inside" is passive — a museum placard, not a trigger. After four sections of escalating tension, it's deceleration at the moment of ignition. The subtitle is worse — explanatory text masquerading as a hook. "Signal profile" is jargon nobody knows.

The rejected "How Much of Your Strategy Actually Lands?" aims evidence at the reader's ego. Every executive believes their strategy is landing. This title says "prove it."

Proposed subtitle: "Configure your org's real structure. See what survives the layers."

The First Principles Thinker — "How Much Signal Survives Your Org?" ★

The real problem isn't the title — it's the transition mechanics. The job of Section 5 isn't to label what they're about to do, it's to make them feel the gap between what they know and what they don't know about their own org.

Title: "How Much Signal Survives Your Org?" — direct callback to Section 1, implies a quantifiable answer they don't have, frames interaction as the only way to find out.

Subtitle: "Set five parameters. See what your structure actually does to strategy, speed, and adaptability." Mechanical on purpose — simple input, concrete payoff.

The Expansionist — "What Shape Are You?"

This is where passive agreement becomes personal stakes. "What Shape Are You?" is short, personal, provocative, and reframes the entire narrative as building toward THIS question.

Subtitle: "Five inputs. Three scores. The physics of your organization — exposed."

The adjacent opportunity: this becomes the thing people say when they share it. Viral framing.

Peer review consensus: weakest response. Abandons physics vocabulary, BuzzFeed quiz tone, title promises a shape classification the tool doesn't deliver.

The Outsider — "How Much Signal Survives Your Structure?"

"Look Inside" is a dead phrase — anatomy textbook energy. Proposes "How Much Signal Survives Your Structure?" which directly references the core concept, frames the tool as personal reckoning, and "survives" has stakes.

Key insight: "Your structure" is tighter than "your organization" and echoes the physics framing. This refinement was adopted in the final recommendation.

Subtitle: "Set five sliders. See what your org actually delivers."

The Executor — "Build Your Signal Profile"

"Fine" isn't enough at this transition. Proposes "Build Your Signal Profile" — active verb, "Build" means they're doing something now. Executives respond to directives, not invitations.

Subtitle: "Set five inputs. See what your structure actually does to information."

Peer review note: Command vs. question tension. Council ruled the question creates a productive beat of discomfort that a command would skip.
