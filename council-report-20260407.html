Consensus: "Resilience" rejected unanimously (5/5). "Speed" rejected or flagged (4/5). "Clarity" split (2 approve, 3 reject). "Agility" endorsed by 3 advisors independently.

Where the Council Agrees

"Resilience" is wrong. Every advisor flagged it independently. A highly damped oscillator that stalls on pivots is the opposite of resilient. The word carries "bouncing back from adversity" connotations that actively mislead users about what the model measures.

"Speed" has a directional problem. The pillar measures delay, not speed. "Latency" is directionally unambiguous — high latency is always bad, no mental inversion required.

Single-word compression is net positive if the words are right. No advisor argued for reverting to two-word names.

Where the Council Clashes

Fidelity vs. Clarity for Pillar 1. The Contrarian and Expansionist prefer "Fidelity" (precise, distinctive, hi-fi audio made it intuitive). The Outsider and Executor prefer "Clarity" (zero hops, instant recognition). The choice turns on primary audience: consultants lean Fidelity, executives lean Clarity.

Blind Spots the Council Caught

The UI's explanatory layer matters. Single-word names don't carry the full diagnostic weight alone. Subtitles and tooltips do real work. "Clarity" paired with "message survival across 5 relays" is different from "Clarity" standing alone.
Audience segmentation was universally missed. C-suite executives need familiar, aspirational vocabulary. Consultants need vocabulary that maps to the model. These are different readers — the right names might differ by context.

The One Thing to Do First

Check your P2 pillar UI. Does it frame the metric as "speed" (higher = better) or "lag" (lower = better)? If the color coding treats high values as green, renaming to "Latency" requires a UI direction flip. Fix the directional convention before locking the name.

Advisor Responses

The Contrarian Voted strongest by all 5 reviewers

The rename has a fatal flaw: none of these three words are specific enough to be diagnostic.

"Clarity" already means a dozen different things in business contexts — communication clarity, strategic clarity, role clarity. Attaching it to "percentage of message that survives hierarchical decay" requires explanation every single time.

"Speed" is generic to the point of meaninglessness. The actual insight — that delay scales quadratically, not linearly — is completely invisible. The word implies a linear problem with a simple fix.

"Resilience" is the most egregious mismatch. Resilience means ability to survive shocks and recover. This measures responsiveness to strategic pivots. A highly damped system that barely moves isn't resilient; it's sluggish.

Alternative: Fidelity, Latency, Responsiveness — still single words, still accessible, but each points at the actual phenomenon.

The First Principles Thinker Challenged the framing

The real problem isn't naming — it's whether the single-word simplification is solving the wrong problem. The two-word names were wrong because they were technical, not because they were two words.

"Clarity" is dangerously misleading — implies the message was well-written, not that structural distortion degrades it. An executive will hear "Clarity" and think "we need better communication." Wrong diagnosis.

Alternative: Reach, Leverage, Agility — each implicitly answers "what happens to strategy in your org?"

The Expansionist Saw the branding opportunity

Single words are the right move, but generic ones leave upside on the table. "Clarity/Speed/Resilience" are on every consulting deck already. They're descriptors, not anchors.

The bigger opportunity is owning words that feel invented for this context but are instantly understood. "Fidelity" is already intuitive (hi-fi audio). "Velocity" implies direction plus speed. "Responsiveness" captures the oscillation insight.

Alternative: Fidelity, Velocity, Responsiveness — positions "Shape Matters" as a precision instrument, not another leadership framework.

The Outsider Fresh eyes on the labels

"Clarity" works for pillar 1. "Speed" is wrong — it measures delay, not speed, creating directional ambiguity. A first-time user seeing "Speed: 47 days" will ask "47 days of what?"

"Resilience" is the biggest mismatch. These three words carry zero distinctiveness — every consulting deck already has a slide with exactly these three words.

Alternative: Clarity, Reach/Lag, Agility/Adaptability

The Executor Ship-focused pragmatist

The rename works with one fix. A VP sees three numbers — with Clarity/Speed/Resilience they immediately know which to show their CEO.

Clarity is accurate. Speed has a directionality issue but works if the UI handles it. Replace Resilience with Agility — it maps directly to "how well does the org respond to a strategic pivot."

Final answer: Clarity, Speed (mind directionality), Agility.

Peer Review Highlights

Unanimous strongest: The Contrarian — identified specific failure modes for each name with concrete alternatives.

Biggest blind spots flagged: The Executor (3 reviewers) for shipping without diagnostic rigor; The First Principles Thinker (2 reviewers) for reframing the question instead of answering it.

What all 5 reviews caught that advisors missed:

  • The UI's explanatory layer (subtitles, tooltips) means single words don't need to carry full precision alone
  • Audience segmentation: executive dashboards vs. practitioner diagnostics may need different vocabulary
  • The single-word constraint itself was never challenged — two-word names were doing real work
