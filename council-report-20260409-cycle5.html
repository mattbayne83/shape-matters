Council Report — Cycle 5 Autoresearch Findings

2026-04-09 · org-shape / Shape Matters · 5 advisors, 5 peer reviews

The question: Are the Cycle 5 findings (signal-decay congestion, DCI sole decorrelation, two-pizza blended model, β(L) power law, DCI calibration) theoretically sound, practically implementable, and worth pursuing? Which should be prioritized for UI vs kept as theoretical backing?

The One Thing to Do First

Build the blended model UI with the teamDecisionMix parameter. This transforms org-shape from a diagnostic tool ("your org is broken, here's a score") into a design tool ("here's what happens if you route decisions differently"). It's the only Cycle 5 finding that changes what a user does after seeing the output.

Chairman's Verdict

Where the Council Agrees

  • The blended model (Finding 3) is the most important output of Cycle 5. All five advisors converged. "Amazon looks broken at 9 layers, but two-pizza teams route 70% of decisions through 2-layer paths, so effective health is 74 not 15." That's a story worth shipping.
  • Findings 1, 4, and 5 are engine internals, not UI features. Congestion model, HHI generalization, and DCI recalibrations make the engine more accurate but exposing them adds complexity without insight.
  • Five autoresearch cycles have reached diminishing returns. "Overfitting the engine while interface questions remain unsolved." The research phase needs a hard stop.

Where the Council Clashes

Is the pillar degeneracy (Finding 2) a crisis or expected behavior?

The Contrarian calls it a bug — if fidelity and authority-agility are perfectly correlated at uniform DCI, three pillars is really two. The peer reviews pushed back hard: fidelity and authority-agility co-moving under uniform conditions is empirically expected. DCI (autonomy) IS the independent axis. The two co-moving pillars reflect reality, not a defect.

Resolution: The degeneracy isn't a bug — but the Contrarian has a residual point: if users see two pillars always moving together, they'll wonder why there are three. The UI needs to frame autonomy as the design lever while fidelity and lag are structural consequences.

Is the congestion model (Finding 1) valuable or overfitting?

The Expansionist sees a publishable empirical law. The Executor sees a correction on a correction. Resolution: keep it in the engine, don't expose it, don't publish until there's theoretical justification for why γ scales with depth.

Blind Spots the Council Caught

  • Validation gap: Six reference companies is interpolation, not validation. The model has never been tested against a real restructuring outcome. One adversarial case would be worth more than another research cycle.
  • Audience comprehension: If the target user (VP, org designer) can't explain the output in a meeting, theoretical soundness is irrelevant. The blended model is the only finding that maps to how leaders think.
  • Sample size is the structural risk, not pillar degeneracy. Six data points can't support the parametric claims being made.

The Recommendation

  1. DCI recalibrations (Finding 5) — Apply immediately. Half a day. No UI changes.
  2. Blended model UI (Finding 3) — The headline feature. teamDecisionMix slider showing before/after health scores.
  3. Congestion & HHI (Findings 1, 4) — Merge into engine. No UI exposure. Document in tech docs.
  4. Pillar presentation — Reframe: Fidelity & Lag are structural consequences, Autonomy is the design lever. Labeling change, not rewrite.
  5. No Cycle 6 autoresearch. Next investment: validation (adversarial case study) or UI/UX.

Full Advisor Responses
The Contrarian

Finding 2 is a red flag, not a finding. Spearman ρ = 1.0 between fidelity and authority-agility at uniform DCI means your model has a structural degeneracy. You've documented a bug and called it a discovery. The fact that only DCI variance breaks the correlation means your three-pillar model is actually a two-pillar model with a cosmetic third axis.

The congestion model is curve-fitting dressed as theory — γ ≈ 0.175 + 0.024L has no organizational interpretation. The two-pizza model is the most dangerous finding — it gives people a knob to confirm their priors.

Finding 4 is solid math but irrelevant to UI. Finding 5 adjustments are trivially small.

Priority: Fix the pillar degeneracy before adding features.

The First Principles Thinker

The core question isn't "are these findings sound?" It's: does this tool need more mathematical precision, or does it need to be more useful?

Finding 2 reveals structural redundancy — three pillars is actually two unless DCI variance exists. Finding 3 (blended model) is the real product. Findings 1, 4, 5 are calibration work.

Red flag: Five cycles of autoresearch on a presentation tool. Diminishing returns are visible — overfitting the engine while interface questions remain unsolved.

Priority: Build blended model UI. Fix pillar redundancy. Stop researching.

The Expansionist

The blended model is the breakout finding. teamDecisionMix turns org-shape from academic curiosity into something a VP of Engineering would use to argue for structural change.

The congestion model is undervalued — γ scaling with depth is an empirical law about organizational bandwidth saturation. Potentially publishable.

DCI variance as sole decorrelation is elegant. Use it to simplify UI — make DCI the primary "personality" slider.

Priority: Blended model UI > congestion viz > DCI as primary slider.

The Outsider

"I don't know what half of this means, and that's the finding." γ, β(L), and HHI are not things product leaders will engage with. They'll nod politely and never open the tool again.

The two-pizza blended model is the only finding that speaks human. DCI recalibration is low-effort, high-trust.

Red flag: Five research cycles and still finding pillars are redundant suggests the model's core dimensionality might be simpler than three pillars.

The Executor

Monday morning list: (1) Ship DCI recalibration — half a day. (2) Ship blended model — 2-3 days, headline feature. (3) Congestion and β(L) as engine internals. (4) DCI decorrelation is diagnostic, not feature.

Red flag: Congestion model's γ varying with L means fitting a correction to a correction.
